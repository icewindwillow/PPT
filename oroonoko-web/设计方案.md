# Oroonoko: Through the Traveller's Eye

## Interactive web presentation design proposal

This document describes a revised design for the English web presentation of Aphra Behn’s *Oroonoko*. It uses the current Git version as the starting point, while allowing individual scenes to be rewritten where the current version compresses the source manuscript too heavily.

The central aim is to make the argument readable as an academic presentation and explorable as a web experience at the same time. The visitor should be able to follow the manuscript from its historical conditions, through the problem of genre, into the three travel-writing features, the close reading of *Oroonoko*, and the final account of its significance.

The final website remains entirely in English. This design document is written in Chinese so that the author can review the structure and content decisions before implementation.

## 1. Content principles

The presentation follows the original manuscript’s five-part argument without turning it into a loose collection of visual slogans:

1. **Historical background:** why eighteenth-century British travel writing flourished.
2. **Blurred generic demarcations:** why travel writing cannot be defined as a closed genre.
3. **Features of travel writing:** authority, empirical description, and cultural encounter.
4. **Case analysis:** how those conventions operate inside *Oroonoko*.
5. **Influence and significance:** what the travel-writing framework reveals about the text.

The original wording will be preserved wherever the manuscript gives a direct quotation or a carefully qualified argument. The web version may divide a long paragraph into readable blocks, but it will not replace the argument with isolated keywords or one-sentence summaries.

All citations will appear directly after the relevant quotation or scholarly claim in the page itself. The format will be consistent with the manuscript, for example:

> “...something like an obligation for the person conscientious about developing the mind and accumulating knowledge” (Buzard 2002: 37)

No important citation will be hidden in an Evidence modal, hover-only label, or secondary popup. The existing Evidence popup and its button should be removed from the final version. Scene navigation may remain, but it is only a way to move through the argument.

The source manuscript gives `Zhang 64–65` without a publication year. The implementation should preserve that information rather than invent a year. If the author supplies the missing year later, it can be inserted consistently as `(Zhang YEAR: 64–65)`.

## 2. Overall visual direction

The visual language keeps the current dark editorial atmosphere: deep blue-black background, warm copper accents, pale manuscript text, and restrained botanical or cartographic textures. The tone should suggest a reading room, a ship’s log, and an annotated eighteenth-century account without becoming a historical costume interface.

The page uses a fixed left navigation rail on desktop and a compact horizontal scene bar on smaller screens. The main content is a sequence of full-height scenes. Each scene has one clear question, one substantial visual or textual interaction, and one visible takeaway.

Typography should continue to distinguish between argument and evidence:

- large serif headings for the main question;
- italic serif treatment for quoted phrases and key conceptual turns;
- restrained sans-serif labels for navigation, metadata, and interaction instructions;
- citation text in the same reading column as the sentence it supports.

The visual system should use motion to clarify a change in argument. Page transitions can use a short crossfade and horizontal drift. Text blocks, citation lines, and evidence cards should enter gently. No animation should move the whole page when the user clicks an in-scene control.

## 3. Navigation and reading model

The presentation has eight scenes. The scene numbers are a web structure, not a replacement for the manuscript’s five-part organization:

| Scene | English label | Manuscript role |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Entry | Framing question and introduction |
| 01 | Conditions | Part I: historical background |
| 02 | Boundaries | Part II: blurred generic demarcations |
| 03 | Authority | Part III Feature 1 and the beginning of Part IV |
| 04 | Description | Part III Feature 2 and the Surinam case |
| 05 | Encounter | Part III Feature 3 and the contact-zone case |
| 06 | Position | The narrator’s gendered and European representational position |
| 07 | Return | Part V: influence and significance |

The author should be able to read every major paragraph by scrolling within the scene. Interaction reveals adjacent text or changes the visible emphasis; it should not be required to access the only copy of an argument.

## 4. Scene-by-scene design

### Scene 00 — Entry: “Through the Traveller’s Eye”

**Purpose:** establish the subject and the guiding question before any theoretical terminology appears.

**Visible English copy:**

> A visual reading of witnessing, describing, and representing a colonial world.
>
> Who gets to describe the world—and whose account becomes history?

The hero image should suggest a river, a journal page, or a distant colonial landscape without illustrating a specific scene from the novel as if it were documentary fact. The title remains the strongest visual element.

The only action on this opening scene is a clear “Enter the manuscript” control. It moves to Scene 01. There is no citation popup and no secondary evidence button.

### Scene 01 — Conditions: “How did travel become knowledge?”

**Purpose:** present the four historical conditions that made eighteenth-century travel writing possible.

**Interaction:** four large cards can be opened in any order. Opening a card adds a full passage to a visible manuscript folio below the cards. The folio grows downward and retains every opened passage, so the user can see accumulation rather than a single changing tooltip.

**Card and passage structure:**

1. **The rise of philosophical empiricism**

   Locke’s tabula-rasa theory positioned sensory experience as a source of knowledge. Travel therefore gained intellectual status as a disciplined way of learning about the world.

   > “an articulated, systematic empiricism made travelling about the world and seeing the new and different ‘something like an obligation for the person conscientious about developing the mind and accumulating knowledge’” (Buzard 2002: 37)

2. **The legacy of Renaissance colonial and maritime exploration**

   Overseas expansion created sustained contact with unfamiliar cultures and a public appetite for travel accounts (Buzard 2002: 37). Early-modern travellers were expected to document foreign observations, establishing documentary conventions for later travel writing (Sherman 2002: 18–20). Hakluyt and Purchas’s travel anthologies helped build a large reading public for overseas narratives.

3. **The Grand Tour and the picturesque aesthetic**

   The Grand Tour functioned as an elite educational rite of passage centred mainly on France and Italy (Buzard 2002: 38). It popularised journals, letters, and travel memoirs. The later picturesque aesthetic extended travel writing to British domestic landscapes and attracted middle-class writers and audiences (Buzard 2002: 38–46).

4. **Generic interplay between travel writing and the early novel**

   Fiction and travel writing shared formal features. Defoe and other authors used the permeability between them to make narrative experience appear empirically grounded.

   > “Travel writing and the novel, especially in its first-person form, have often shared a focus on the centrality of the self, a concern with empirical detail, and a movement through time and place which is simply sequential.” (Hulme & Youngs 2002: 5)

The completed folio ends with the original synthesis: empiricist epistemology, colonial and cross-cultural encounters, Grand-Tour customs, and literary exchange together produced the conditions for the prosperity of eighteenth-century British travel writing.

### Scene 02 — Boundaries: “Where does a genre begin?”

**Purpose:** make generic fluidity something the visitor discovers through classification rather than something the page merely announces.

**Interaction:** three overlapping transparent regions are labelled:

- `Documentary Record`
- `Travel Narrative`
- `Fiction`

Four feature cards can be dragged into one or more regions. The same card may be placed in multiple regions. A click-to-select and click-to-place fallback is required for touch screens and keyboard users.

The cards are:

- `First-person narration`
- `Eyewitness claims`
- `Detailed descriptions`
- `Movement through places`

When a feature appears in more than one region, the interface draws a visible connection between those regions and labels the shared technique. The card is never marked simply “wrong”; the overlap is the point of the exercise.

The explanatory copy beneath the map should retain the manuscript’s definition of the problem:

> Travel writing functions as an umbrella term covering travel accounts, journals, letters, historical records, and fictional narratives; its heterogeneity frustrates rigid generic demarcation (Borm 2004: 13).

The page should also state that modern scholarship treats travel writing as a hybrid formation produced through the interaction of factual representation and literary-rhetorical construction (Tian 2024: 152).

The next paragraph should retain the broader working definition:

> Travel writing cannot be confined by narrow prescriptive criteria; researchers must rely on a broad working definition rather than a closed generic model (Hulme & Youngs 2002: 1).

The overlap map then leads to the manuscript’s method:

> Instead of asking what travel writing is, we can ask what it does.

The page ends by naming the recurring characteristics that the rest of the presentation will follow: authenticity and eyewitness authority, empirical description and observation, and cultural encounter and the representation of the Other.

### Scene 03 — Authority: “Who gets to be a witness?”

**Purpose:** introduce the first feature of travel writing and show how narrative authority is assembled.

**Interaction:** a three-step authority chain is visible on the page:

1. `Being there` — on-site presence
2. `Claiming truth` — firsthand observation
3. `Preserving a story` — a history left out

Activating a step adds its evidence to a visible “witness record” below the chain. The user can activate all three and see how the claim develops from physical presence, to truth claim, to historical necessity. The evidence remains inline; there is no popup.

**Theoretical text:**

Travel writing builds textual authority through rhetorical authenticity and first-person eyewitness authority (Zhang 64–65). Early-modern writers framed their accounts as firsthand observation and true testimony in order to persuade audiences. Mary Louise Pratt calls the normative white male European observing subject the “seeing-man”: a figure who exercises representational power through performed on-site witnessing within the colonial contact zone (Pratt 2008: 9–10).

**Case evidence from *Oroonoko*:**

The narrator deliberately frames the story as a true history based on personal observation and authentic testimony. Her narrative “I” operates as an authority marker. Her physical presence in Surinam provides eyewitness proof and positions her as an intermediary connecting European readers with an unfamiliar overseas space.

The page should preserve the key textual phrases as large pull quotes, with the analysis immediately below each one:

> “Truth” rather than fabricated adventures.

> “only a female pen”

The first quotation establishes the narrator’s resistance to fictional invention. The second creates a productive tension: female authorship may appear to weaken authority within an eighteenth-century male-centred literary culture, yet the absence of official accounts makes Behn the custodian of Oroonoko’s otherwise-lost history.

The section should not resolve the tension into a simple celebration. It should show both the constraint and the resource of Behn’s position.

### Scene 04 — Description: “When a landscape becomes knowledge”

**Purpose:** show that description is a knowledge-making act rather than neutral decoration.

**Interaction:** a landscape image is paired with a three-step reading sequence:

1. `Observe`
2. `Describe`
3. `Classify`

The first step reveals tags for flora, local custom, and natural resource. The second overlays short descriptive fragments. The third turns the image into a catalogue or field record and displays a note explaining that classification makes an unfamiliar environment legible through the observer’s framework.

**Theoretical text:**

European travel writing relies on empirical observation and detailed description. Travellers record landscapes, flora and fauna, local manners, and customs through direct encounters. These accounts transform contact with unfamiliar places into organised knowledge for home audiences, while still carrying Euro-centred assumptions (Pratt 2008: 24–31; Thompson 2011: 27–29).

The page should retain the manuscript’s distinction between factual surface and interpretive frame: empirical observation supplies the factual surface, but description itself produces knowledge and is shaped by the observer’s cultural standpoint.

**Case evidence:**

Behn constructs Surinam as an observable and classifiable space through detailed descriptions of its inhabitants, animals, natural resources, and colonial landscapes. The accumulation of visual details creates the impression of direct observation.

The European comparison must remain visible as a two-part textual sequence:

> “about half the length of the Mall here”

The grove becomes measurable through a familiar European urban reference.

> “Not all the Gardens of boasted Italy can produce a Shade to out-vie this”

The landscape is evaluated through European aesthetic standards, even when the comparison allows Surinam to surpass the celebrated European spaces.

These quotations should be shown as the user progresses through the landscape, with the original analysis beside them rather than replaced by a short label.

### Scene 05 — Encounter: “When the gaze turns back”

**Purpose:** explain the contact zone and the partial reciprocity of colonial encounter.

**Interaction:** the scene begins as a split view: `European visitor / looking outward` and `Indigenous observers / looking back`. A `Reverse the gaze` control shifts the visual emphasis and changes the caption. The text below remains visible throughout, so the interaction clarifies an argument instead of hiding it.

**Theoretical text:**

> “By ‘contact zone,’ I use to refer to the space of imperial encounters, the space in which peoples geographically and historically separated come into contact with each other and establish ongoing relations, usually involving conditions of coercion, radical inequality, and intractable conflict.” (Pratt 2008: 7)

The page should then explain Pratt’s traveller/travelee pairing and the unequal co-presence of European visitors and local communities (Pratt 2008: 7). It should retain the discussion of transculturation and autoethnographic counter-narratives (Pratt 2008: 8–9).

**Case evidence:**

Indigenous people are not presented only as passive objects of observation. Their curiosity makes Europeans visible within the encounter. At the same time, their reactions are filtered through the European narrator’s perspective, so reciprocity does not erase unequal representational power.

The page should explicitly connect this scene with Pratt’s account of the “seeing-man” and anti-conquest: European observers can present themselves as innocent witnesses while still turning colonial subjects into textual objects for metropolitan readers (Pratt 2008: 9, 51–52).

### Scene 06 — Position: “A narrator between frameworks”

**Purpose:** hold together the two positions that the manuscript refuses to simplify.

**Interaction:** two large frames can be activated independently:

- `Gender and authorship`
- `Europe and representation`

When only one is active, the page displays that perspective. When both are active, a central statement appears:

> A narrator can challenge one hierarchy while remaining within another.

The first frame shows how a woman writer unsettles the exclusivity of masculine travel authority. The second shows that colonial subjects are still interpreted through a European cultural framework. The page should present these as simultaneous conditions, not as mutually exclusive verdicts.

### Scene 07 — Return: “What the story leaves behind”

**Purpose:** return from the individual scenes to the argument of the whole presentation.

The final page should use three visible statements rather than three empty navigation cards:

1. **Authority** — Travel-writing conventions help Behn establish credibility through witnessing and testimony.
2. **Knowledge** — Description transforms unfamiliar landscapes and peoples into forms legible to European readers, while carrying the observer’s cultural assumptions.
3. **Representation** — Cultural encounters are remembered through a narrator who can acknowledge reciprocity while still controlling the final interpretation.

The final synthesis should remain close to the original manuscript:

> Travel writing provides a useful framework for understanding *Oroonoko* not simply as a fictional account of slavery, but as a text shaped by early modern practices of witnessing, describing, and representing foreign worlds.

The closing screen should not add a new theoretical claim. It should leave the visitor with the manuscript’s final relationship between authority, colonial knowledge, and cultural encounter.

## 6. Interaction and accessibility requirements

- Every interaction has a visible text explanation before it is used.
- Every click-based interaction has a keyboard and touch fallback.
- Dragging is optional; selection and placement must work without drag-and-drop.
- The complete argument remains readable without activating any control.
- No citation is revealed only by hover, popup, or modal window.
- Buttons use clear English labels and visible active states.
- Focus states must remain visible for keyboard navigation.
- Motion is short and local to the changed component; clicking a card must not replay the whole page entrance animation.
- At reduced width, cards stack vertically and text remains readable without horizontal scrolling.

## 7. Image and animation direction

The presentation can use one consistent generated or licensed visual language:

- a restrained Surinam river and vegetation image for the opening and description scenes;
- transparent manuscript paper, marginal marks, and route lines for transitions;
- a split visual for the contact-zone scene;
- subtle paper overlays when a quotation enters the reading column.

Images should be atmospheric and interpretive. They should not be presented as documentary portraits of Oroonoko, Indigenous people, or historical events unless the source and status of the image are clearly identified.

Animation should support the argument:

- cards add passages to the folio;
- overlap lines appear when a technique belongs to several regions;
- the authority chain lights up as evidence accumulates;
- observation becomes description and classification;
- the gaze reverses in the encounter scene;
- the two positional frames converge in the synthesis scene.

## 8. Review and acceptance criteria

The author review version should be judged on the following points:

1. The five-part argument is recognisable from the scene sequence.
2. The full case-analysis logic is present, including the quoted phrases and their explanations.
3. Every author/year/page citation supplied by the manuscript appears beside the relevant claim.
4. The page does not hide essential scholarship inside a popup or secondary evidence control.
5. Interactions reveal relationships and accumulated evidence rather than replacing the text with decorative movement.
6. The final website remains English-language, visually coherent, keyboard-usable, and readable on a normal laptop screen.

# 中文对照版

## 互动网页展示设计方案

本方案以当前 Git 版本为基础，重新规划 Aphra Behn《Oroonoko》的英文互动网页展示。页面最终仍然使用英文，但这部分中文用于帮助作者快速审阅内容结构、学术论证和引用处理方式。

整体目标是让网页同时具备两种功能：它首先是一份可以完整阅读的学术展示，其次才是一种具有探索感的互动网页。观众应当能够沿着原稿的论证顺序阅读：先理解十八世纪旅行写作兴起的历史条件，再理解文类边界为什么模糊，随后进入旅行写作的三个核心特征，最后回到《Oroonoko》的具体案例和意义。

## 一、内容原则

网页严格遵循原稿的五部分结构：

1. **时代背景**：十八世纪英国旅行写作为什么兴盛。
2. **文类边界的模糊性**：为什么旅行写作无法被定义为一个封闭、固定的文类。
3. **旅行写作的三个特征**：叙述权威、经验性描述、跨文化遭遇与差异再现。
4. **具体案例分析**：这些旅行写作惯例如何在《Oroonoko》中运作。
5. **意义与影响**：旅行写作视角如何帮助我们重新理解这部作品。

原稿中的直接引文、限定性表述和学者观点都应保留。网页可以把长段落拆成几个容易阅读的文本块，但不能把完整论证压缩成关键词或一句结论。

引用直接放在相关句子或引文之后，使用统一的英文格式，例如：

> “...something like an obligation for the person conscientious about developing the mind and accumulating knowledge” (Buzard 2002: 37)

引用不再藏在 Evidence 弹窗、悬停标签或其他二级窗口中。最终版本应移除 Evidence 弹窗及其按钮。页面导航可以保留，但它只负责在论证场景之间移动。

原稿中出现了 `Zhang 64–65`，但没有提供年份。网页不应自行猜测年份。如果作者之后补充年份，再统一改成 `(Zhang YEAR: 64–65)`。

## 二、整体视觉方向

整体视觉沿用当前版本的深蓝黑背景、铜色强调、浅色手稿文字和克制的植物或地图纹理。氛围可以同时让人联想到阅览室、航海日志和十八世纪旅行记录，但不要做成单纯的历史装饰页面。

桌面端使用左侧固定导航栏，小屏幕使用横向紧凑的场景栏。主内容由八个全屏场景组成。每一页都应有一个明确的问题、一个服务于论证的视觉或文字互动，以及一个清晰可见的阶段性结论。

字体继续区分论证与证据：大号衬线字体呈现核心问题，斜体衬线字体呈现引文和概念转折，无衬线字体用于导航、元数据和操作提示，引用与被支持的句子放在同一阅读栏中。

页面切换可以使用短暂的淡入和横向移动。文本、引用和证据卡片可以轻柔进入，但点击页面内的控制项时不能让整页重新抖动或重复播放入场动画。

## 三、导航和阅读方式

网页保留八个场景。八个场景是网页上的阅读结构，同时对应原稿的五个部分：

| 场景 | 英文标签 | 对应原稿内容 |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Entry | 开场问题和研究框架 |
| 01 | Conditions | 第一部分：时代背景 |
| 02 | Boundaries | 第二部分：文类边界模糊 |
| 03 | Authority | 第三部分特征一，以及第四部分开头 |
| 04 | Description | 第三部分特征二，以及 Surinam 景观案例 |
| 05 | Encounter | 第三部分特征三，以及接触区案例 |
| 06 | Position | Behn 的性别位置与欧洲再现框架 |
| 07 | Return | 第五部分：意义与影响 |

观众不需要点击任何按钮才能读到主要论证。互动只能显示相邻解释、改变文本强调或让证据累积，不应成为获取唯一正文的门槛。

## 四、逐页设计

### 第 00 页：Entry — “Through the Traveller’s Eye”

这一页负责建立主题和核心问题，不要一开始就堆叠理论术语。

页面英文文案：

> A visual reading of witnessing, describing, and representing a colonial world.
>
> Who gets to describe the world—and whose account becomes history?

开场图像可以使用河流、手稿或遥远的殖民地景观，不能把某一幅图像伪装成作品中的历史事实。标题仍然是页面最强的视觉元素。

这一页只需要一个明确的 `Enter the manuscript` 控制项，进入第一场景。不要放 Evidence 弹窗或其他二级证据按钮。

### 第 01 页：Conditions — “How did travel become knowledge?”

这一页呈现旅行写作兴起的四个历史条件。四张卡片可以按任意顺序打开，每打开一张，就把完整段落加入下面的 Manuscript Folio。已经打开的段落保留在页面中，让观众看到历史条件逐步累积，而不是只看到一条替换后的提示语。

四张卡片分别是：

1. **The rise of philosophical empiricism**

   Locke 的白板说把感官经验置于知识来源的位置。旅行因此获得了知识活动的地位，可以被理解为一种有纪律的学习方式。

   > “an articulated, systematic empiricism made travelling about the world and seeing the new and different ‘something like an obligation for the person conscientious about developing the mind and accumulating knowledge’” (Buzard 2002: 37)

2. **The legacy of Renaissance colonial and maritime exploration**

   海外扩张造成了与陌生文化的持续接触，也制造了公众对旅行记述的需求 (Buzard 2002: 37)。早期现代旅行者需要记录外国见闻，这些记录逐渐建立了之后旅行写作的纪实惯例 (Sherman 2002: 18–20)。Hakluyt 和 Purchas 的旅行文集帮助海外叙事形成了更大的读者群。

3. **The Grand Tour and the picturesque aesthetic**

   Grand Tour 是一种以法国和意大利为中心的精英教育仪式 (Buzard 2002: 38)。它普及了日记、书信和旅行回忆录。之后的 picturesque aesthetic 又把旅行写作的对象扩展到英国本土景观，并吸引了中产阶级作者和读者 (Buzard 2002: 38–46)。

4. **Generic interplay between travel writing and the early novel**

   小说和旅行写作共享多种形式特征。Defoe 等作家利用两者之间的流动边界，让文学叙事也具有经验事实的外观。

   > “Travel writing and the novel, especially in its first-person form, have often shared a focus on the centrality of the self, a concern with empirical detail, and a movement through time and place which is simply sequential.” (Hulme & Youngs 2002: 5)

   四个条件全部打开后，手稿页的结论应保留原稿含义：经验主义认识论、殖民和跨文化遭遇、Grand Tour 习俗，以及旅行写作与小说之间的文学交换，共同构成了十八世纪英国旅行写作兴盛的文化条件。

### 第 02 页：Boundaries — “Where does a genre begin?”

这一页把“文类流动性”变成观众亲自发现的问题。

画面中出现三个彼此重叠的透明区域：

- `Documentary Record`
- `Travel Narrative`
- `Fiction`

四张特征卡可以拖入一个或多个区域：

- `First-person narration`
- `Eyewitness claims`
- `Detailed descriptions`
- `Movement through places`

同一张卡可以放入多个区域。点击选择、再点击区域的操作也必须可用，以支持触屏和键盘。卡片不应被简单判定为错误，因为共享本身就是这一页要展示的结论。

当一个特征进入多个区域时，界面显示连接线和共享技术标签。地图下方直接显示原稿对问题的解释：

> Travel writing functions as an umbrella term covering travel accounts, journals, letters, historical records, and fictional narratives; its heterogeneity frustrates rigid generic demarcation (Borm 2004: 13).

还要保留现代研究的判断：旅行写作是由事实再现和文学修辞建构共同形成的混合形态 (Tian 2024: 152)。

页面随后呈现广义工作定义：旅行写作不能由狭窄的规定性标准限制，研究者需要一个宽泛的工作定义，而不是封闭的文类模型 (Hulme & Youngs 2002: 1)。

最后引出原稿的方法：

> Instead of asking what travel writing is, we can ask what it does.

并明确接下来的三个分析特征：真实性与目击者权威、经验性描述与观察、跨文化遭遇与对他者的再现。

### 第 03 页：Authority — “Who gets to be a witness?”

这一页介绍旅行写作的第一个特征：叙述权威如何被建构。

画面显示一个三段式 authority chain：

1. `Being there` — on-site presence
2. `Claiming truth` — firsthand observation
3. `Preserving a story` — a history left out

每激活一个节点，就把对应证据加入下面的 witness record。三个节点全部激活后，观众可以看到权威如何从现场存在，发展为真理主张，最后变成保存被官方历史遗漏的故事的必要性。证据直接出现在正文中，不使用弹窗。

理论段落应保留：旅行写作通过修辞真实性和第一人称目击者权威建立文本权威 (Zhang 64–65)。早期现代作家把自己的叙述包装成亲历观察和真实证词，以说服读者。Pratt 用 “seeing-man” 描述在殖民接触区中通过现场见证行使再现权力的规范性白人欧洲男性观察者 (Pratt 2008: 9–10)。

案例部分要保留原稿的文本主体，并把关键短语作为大号引文显示：

> “Truth” rather than fabricated adventures.

> “only a female pen”

第一句说明叙述者如何拒绝被看成虚构发明。第二句形成双重张力：女性作者身份在十八世纪男性中心的文学文化中可能削弱权威，但官方历史对 Oroonoko 的缺席又让 Behn 成为保存这段遗失历史的人。

页面应同时保留这种身份的限制和资源，不能把复杂矛盾简化成单一的赞美结论。

### 第 04 页：Description — “When a landscape becomes knowledge”

这一页说明描述并不是装饰，而是生产知识的行为。

互动分成三个阶段：

1. `Observe`
2. `Describe`
3. `Classify`

第一阶段在图像上显示 flora、local custom、natural resource 等标签；第二阶段叠加短文本描述；第三阶段把图像转成类似目录或田野记录的结构，并解释分类如何通过观察者的框架让陌生环境变得可理解。

理论段落应说明：欧洲旅行写作依赖经验观察和细节描述。旅行者通过直接接触记录景观、动植物、当地习俗和社会行为。这些叙述把陌生空间组织成面向本土读者的知识，同时携带以欧洲为中心的假设 (Pratt 2008: 24–31; Thompson 2011: 27–29)。

案例分析要保留：Behn 通过对居民、动物、自然资源和殖民景观的目录式描述，把 Surinam 建构为可观察、可分类的空间。细节的累积制造出直接观察的印象。

两个欧洲比较必须作为完整的文本序列显示：

> “about half the length of the Mall here”

这句话用欧洲读者熟悉的城市参照物衡量树林的长度。

> “Not all the Gardens of boasted Italy can produce a Shade to out-vie this”

这句话把 Surinam 放在欧洲审美标准中衡量，同时又允许殖民地景观超过著名的欧洲园林。

观众推进景观阶段时，旁边显示原文和分析，不能只剩下 `observe / describe / classify` 三个标签。

### 第 05 页：Encounter — “When the gaze turns back”

这一页解释接触区以及殖民遭遇中的有限互惠性。

开始时画面分成两部分：`European visitor / looking outward` 和 `Indigenous observers / looking back`。点击 `Reverse the gaze` 后，视觉重心和说明文字发生变化。但下面的理论解释一直可见，确保互动是在帮助理解，而不是隐藏文本。

接触区的定义应保留原文：

> “By ‘contact zone,’ I use to refer to the space of imperial encounters, the space in which peoples geographically and historically separated come into contact with each other and establish ongoing relations, usually involving conditions of coercion, radical inequality, and intractable conflict.” (Pratt 2008: 7)

页面还要解释 Pratt 对 traveller 和 travelee 的配对，以及欧洲访问者和当地社群之间不平等的共同在场 (Pratt 2008: 7)。同时保留 transculturation 和 autoethnographic counter-narratives 的讨论 (Pratt 2008: 8–9)。

案例部分要说明：当地人的好奇使欧洲人也成为被观看者，因此遭遇不是单向窗口。但当地人的反应最终仍然经过欧洲叙述者的视角，互惠性没有消除再现权力的不平等。

页面应把这一案例连接到 Pratt 关于 “seeing-man” 和 anti-conquest 的讨论：欧洲观察者可以把自己呈现为无辜的见证者，同时仍然把殖民地主体转化为面向本土读者的文本对象 (Pratt 2008: 9, 51–52)。

### 第 06 页：Position — “A narrator between frameworks”

这一页保留原稿拒绝简单化的两个位置。

观众可以分别激活两个框架：

- `Gender and authorship`
- `Europe and representation`

只激活一个时，页面显示对应的分析；两个都激活后，中间出现：

> A narrator can challenge one hierarchy while remaining within another.

第一部分说明女性作者如何动摇男性旅行权威的排他性。第二部分说明殖民地主体仍然通过欧洲文化框架被解释。两者应同时存在，而不能互相抵消。

### 第 07 页：Return — “What the story leaves behind”

最后一页回到整篇论证，不再显示空的导航卡片，而是显示三个明确结论：

1. **Authority**：旅行写作惯例帮助 Behn 通过见证和证词建立可信度。
2. **Knowledge**：描述把陌生景观和人群转化为欧洲读者可以理解的形式，同时携带观察者的文化假设。
3. **Representation**：文化遭遇可以承认互惠性，但最终仍由叙述者控制解释和记忆方式。

最终总结应贴近原稿：

> Travel writing provides a useful framework for understanding *Oroonoko* not simply as a fictional account of slavery, but as a text shaped by early modern practices of witnessing, describing, and representing foreign worlds.

结尾不新增理论判断，而是回到原稿关于叙述权威、殖民知识和文化遭遇之间关系的结论。

## 六、互动和无障碍要求

- 每个互动项在使用前都要有清楚的英文说明。
- 点击互动必须有键盘和触屏替代方式。
- 拖放不是必需操作，卡片应支持“点击选择—点击放置”。
- 不激活任何按钮时，完整论证仍然可读。
- 引用不能只在悬停、弹窗或模态窗口中出现。
- 所有按钮都要使用明确的英文标签，并显示激活状态。
- 键盘操作时必须保留清晰的 focus 状态。
- 动画只作用于发生变化的局部组件，点击卡片不能让整页重新播放入场动画。
- 小屏幕下卡片应纵向排列，正文保持可读，不依赖横向滚动。

## 七、图片和动画方向

可以使用统一的生成图或授权图像风格：

- 开场和描述页使用 Surinam 河流与植物的克制图像；
- 页面切换使用透明手稿纸、边注和路线线条；
- Encounter 页使用左右分割的观看关系；
- 引文进入阅读栏时使用轻微的纸张叠印效果。

图像应该具有氛围和解释功能，不应把 Oroonoko、当地人或历史事件画成未经说明的“真实历史肖像”。

动画应服务于论证：条件卡片把段落加入手稿，文类卡片在多个区域之间产生连接线，权威链随着证据累积逐步点亮，景观由观察进入描述和分类，Encounter 页让观看方向翻转，Position 页让两个框架最终汇合。

6. 最终网站保持英文，视觉统一，支持键盘操作，并能在普通笔记本屏幕上清楚阅读。
